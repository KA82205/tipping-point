About this site

Tipping Point is a personal, non-commercial project. It runs the State Presidential Approval/State Economy Model created by Peter K. Enns (Cornell University and Verasight), Jonathan Colner, Anusha Kumar and Julius Lagodny, published in PS: Political Science & Politics. The model and its published forecasts are theirs. This site is not affiliated with or endorsed by the authors, and any errors in the code are its own.

The idea: presidential elections are largely decided by fundamentals that are knowable months ahead. The authors publish their forecast about 100 days before Election Day. The name comes from the state that decides the Electoral College, and the logo is that idea drawn as districts on a balance beam.

The 2024 scorecard compares the authors' published July 2024 forecast with the certified results, state by state. The forecast numbers are read from the paper's own charts.

2028 today runs the same equation every morning on the latest Trump approval polls and state economic data, and lets you try any matchup. It shows what the model says if the election were held today. It is not the forecast, which needs the nominees and June–July 2028 surveys and is due July 30, 2028.

Everything updates automatically: polls, state economies, and the nominees, running mates and home states once the conventions pick them. Data: FRED (Philadelphia Fed state coincident indexes), VoteHub and PollResults.org (poll data, CC BY 4.0), Wikipedia, certified 2024 returns.

What the model reads

Eight variables in all, three of which do most of the work. Each number is how many points of Democratic vote share one unit of the input is worth.

1.43

State economy

Per point of weighted monthly growth in the Philadelphia Fed's coincident index, with recent quarters counting most.

    How the model works

    The State Presidential Approval/State Economy Model by Peter K. Enns, Jonathan Colner, Anusha Kumar and Julius Lagodny predicts the Democratic share of the two-party vote in every state, then simulates the Electoral College 70,000 times.

    Democratic two-party share in a state = 49.38 + 0.82 × state deviation from the national vote, previous election + 0.33 × state presidential approval (signed toward the Democrat) + 1.43 × weighted change in the state coincident economic index + 2.47 × presidential candidate's home state − 3.49 × presidential candidate's home state, previous election + 1.87 × running mate's home state − 0.35 × Anderson 1980 share (1984 only) − 0.60 × Perot 1996 share (2000 only)

    Fitted on 11 elections × 51 jurisdictions, 1980–2020 (N = 561). Adjusted R² 0.90, standard error 3.46 points.

    Uncertainty

    Each forecast runs 10,000 draws of the coefficients, adds model error with a standard deviation equal to the model's root mean square error, and repeats across seven values of the economic weighting parameter: 70,000 simulations.

    Maine and Nebraska districts take the statewide forecast plus each district's gap from its state in the previous election.

    Ratings

    Solid when a candidate wins more than 98 in 100 simulations, Likely above 75, Lean above 55, Toss-up otherwise.

    The 2024 scorecard's state forecasts and 90% ranges are read from the paper's Figure A-2, which plots each state's simulated distribution; the recovered values reproduce every call and rating in the paper.

    Data behind 2028 today

    State economies come from the Philadelphia Fed State Coincident Indexes via FRED. Trump's national approval is an average of public polls from VoteHub and PollResults.org. Until state-level estimates are available, each state's approval is the national average shifted by its 2024 Trump margin, pulled toward any state polls.

    Nominees, running mates and their home states are read from Wikipedia each morning. The vice-president adjustment uses favorability, as the authors did for 1988, because no free source asks about vice-presidential approval.

    A few settings the paper does not publish, such as the approval baseline and the economic weighting parameter, use values consistent with it and will be checked against the replication data.
